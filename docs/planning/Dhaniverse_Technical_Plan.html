Dhaniverse · technical plan

Dhaniverse Technical Plan

Learn money, learn finance, earn money. The full system: sponsor campaigns, an energy economy priced by Uniswap, a house reward pool, yield on idle money, and a safety net so nobody ever gets stuck.

Written 5 Nov 2026, before the first phase Chain: Ethereum Sepolia Replaces the earlier Arc-based rewards plan

The system in one screen

  1. Sponsors fund campaigns. Their USDC sits in an escrow contract. Their quests appear on the Bounty Board as story missions that make players really use the sponsor's product, such as swapping on Uniswap inside the game.
  2. Quests cost energy, and energy comes from food. Food costs a tiny amount of USDC, a few cents for a full bar. Players never hold food tokens: the food is bought and burned in the same transaction, and the player only gets energy.
  3. Food prices move with supply and demand on a Uniswap v4 pool. Every meal anyone buys pushes the price up; our farm's harvest pushes it down. A hook keeps it inside a floor and a ceiling.
  4. Food money funds the house reward pool. That pool pays for our own fun: chests, streak bonuses, Market Rush prizes, mystery quests and rescue meals. It spends 4% of its balance a day, so it can never run dry.
  5. Idle sponsor money earns yield. We keep a cash buffer sized from the campaigns' daily caps and put the rest in a no-lock-in vault. The interest goes to the house pool.
  6. Nobody can get stuck. Free energy regenerates, there's a free daily meal, every paid quest earns at least four times its food cost, and a broke player gets a rescue meal.
  7. USDC is the only money. There are no coins, no play money and no life sim (no wages, rent, savings or loans). Every amount a player sees, pays or earns is real USDC.

Money

USDC, and nothing else

Buys food and therefore energy. Earned from sponsored quests and house rewards. Lives in the player's wallet. Shown in blue with a dollar sign everywhere. There are no coins, no points that can be spent, no play money and no life sim: no wages, rent, savings or loans.

What a player can earn that isn't money: energy, meal vouchers (thalis bought for them with real USDC from a pool), tickets for the weekly draw, titles and stamps on their ENS name. None of these can be bought, sold or turned into USDC.

Uniswap

How the food price follows supply and demand

We already built most of this: FoodToken, FoodHook and FoodRouter in contracts/src/food/. They were written for Arc and move to Sepolia with small changes.

  1. One pool, FOOD/USDC, on Uniswap v4. The hook lets only our router trade or add stock, so outsiders can't move the price. It keeps the price between a floor and a ceiling (0.5× and 2× base) and limits each day's move. Widen today's 5% daily limit to 15% so the price visibly flickers.
  2. Demand: every meal is a buy. A new MealCounter contract holds the router's Buyer role. The player's wallet calls buyMeal(ticket): it takes the USDC, buys FOOD through the pool, burns it in the same transaction, and emits MealEaten. The server sees the event and adds energy. The player never holds FOOD, so there's nothing to trade.
  3. Supply: the farm sells the harvest. A keeper with the Seller role sells new FOOD into the pool through the day. Harvest size responds to price, so supply chases demand and the price drifts back toward base.
    harvest_today = base_harvest × (1 + 0.5 × (yesterday's avg price ÷ base − 1)), clamped to 0.5×–2× story shocks: drought −30% · festival demand · bumper crop +40% (the morning paper reports them)
  4. The price ticks with the whole town. Every meal anyone buys moves it; each harvest tranche pulls it back. The market board in the square shows the live price and history (read from Arkiv).
  5. Food can never sell out. When the pool is at the ceiling, MealCounter falls back to the ration shop: it sells from a reserve at the ceiling price. That also teaches price controls.
  6. Server-signed meal tickets. Each purchase needs an EIP-712 ticket from the server {player, meal, maxUsdc, expiry, ref}. That stops bots buying outside the game, and blocks buying when energy is already full, so nobody can hoard.

Who gets the food money?

We're the only seller, so all the USDC players spend ends up with the farm when it sells FOOD into the pool. The farm forwards it to the Treasury, which splits it: 60% house pool, 10% safety net, 20% operations (paymaster gas, servers) and 10% team. The pool itself charges no fee.

Safety net

Making sure nobody ever gets stuck

The deadlock you described is real: prices are shared, so one player can be priced out by everyone else. Five rules make it impossible, and the server checks each one.

House reward pool

How we pay for our own fun

daily house budget = 4% × pool balance (it can never reach zero; it shrinks by half in ~17 days with no income) steady pool size = daily inflow ÷ 0.04 ($50 a day in → the pool settles at about $1,250)
Share of the daily budgetSpent onBucket

Why two buckets: food revenue, which comes from players' own money, funds only the fixed rewards. Random rewards (chests, golden drops, mystery boxes) are funded only by sponsor fees, sponsor drops and yield. Nothing random is ever paid for by what a player spent, and chests are never sold. See Risks for why.

Yield

Earning interest on idle sponsor money

The key fact: every campaign has a daily payout cap, so the most money that can leave in any three days is known in advance. The buffer is sized from that, and the rest earns interest.

buffer = 1.5 × 3 days × (sum of live campaigns' daily caps) + refunds requested invest = min(80% of float, float − buffer) rebalance every hour: liquid < 0.5 × buffer → withdraw up to buffer liquid > 1.5 × buffer → deposit the excess (within the 80% cap) yield = vault assets − what we owe sponsors → 80% house pool, 20% loss reserve (reserve stops filling at 2% of the invested amount; then 100% to the pool)
Idle float investedPer day at 4% a yearPer month
$10,000$1.10$33
$100,000$11$329
$1,000,000$110$3,288

Worked example: live campaigns cap out at $500 a day, so the buffer is 1.5 × 3 × $500 = $2,250. With $20,000 of float we invest min($16,000, $17,750) = $16,000, which earns about $1.75 a day.

No lock-ins, ever

Only venues you can withdraw from in the same block: Aave v3 USDC supply, or ERC-4626 vaults with instant redemption. Never staking or fixed terms. Split across two venues.

What if we need the money?

The buffer covers three days of the maximum possible payouts. If a venue's withdrawals stall (Aave at 100% utilisation), we draw from the other. In the worst case claims queue for up to 24 hours, as the sponsor terms say.

Whose interest is it?

The campaign terms say Dhaniverse keeps the yield and the sponsor's principal is always owed first. Later option: rebate half to the sponsor as extra budget, so their campaign runs longer.

On testnet

Aave's Sepolia market uses its own test USDC, not Circle's. So we demo with a mock ERC-4626 vault that accrues a stated rate, clearly labelled simulated. The keeper and accounting are real.

Honest scale: yield is a bonus, not the engine, until float reaches six figures. Sponsors are the engine; food is the price game and the money sink.

The flagship: the Spice Run (Uniswap)

  1. The harbourmaster gives you cargo: carry 0.50 USDC to the spice market and buy MANGO. If your wallet has less, he advances it from the campaign. One open advance per human, repaid automatically when you deliver. It's a micro-loan lesson.
  2. At the exchange kiosk you swap for real. The kiosk shows the Uniswap quote, the fee and slippage in plain words. You confirm, and a real swap runs through Uniswap's Universal Router from your own wallet, with gas covered.
  3. Deliver to the spice merchant at spice-merchant.tallyport.eth. The delivery contract checks you brought at least the quoted MANGO. It pays back your 0.50 cargo plus the 0.50 reward from escrow, and repays any advance first.
  4. The server confirms the swap from the chain. It looks for the Swap event by your account inside the quest window, then marks the quest done and posts it to the feed.

The sponsor gets a verified human who made a real Uniswap swap, real buy volume on their token, and a reason to come back. For the demo MANGO is our own token with a seeded v4 pool; in production it's the sponsor's real token.

What sponsors see on their dashboard

Verified unique humans reached, completed quests, on-chain actions and volume, cost per verified action, and whether those players used the product again on their own after 7 and 30 days. That last number is what quest platforms can't show, and it's our pitch to sponsors.

Problems you didn't list

Risks and fixes

The big one: Indian law

India's Promotion and Regulation of Online Gaming Act, 2025 (rules in force since 1 May 2026) bans online money games: games where a player pays, deposits or stakes something hoping to win money, whether by skill or chance. Paying USDC for energy that's used to earn USDC could be read that way. This plan reduces the risk: energy is always free to get, walking, talking and Rush runs are free, contests cost no energy, random rewards are never funded by player money, and nothing random is sold. It does not remove the risk. Testnet at the build is fine. Before real money, get a lawyer's opinion, and be ready to launch outside India or switch paid food off there. This isn't legal advice.
