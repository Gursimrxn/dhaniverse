# Dhaniverse world and play design proposal

**5 November 2026. World layout, art and activity proposal.**

**Gameplay update 5 November 2026:** [Player choices and consequences](14_Player_Choices_And_Consequences.md) governs the next playable milestone. The daily routine below is one useful route and verification scenario. It is not a mandatory itinerary: the same neighborhood must support different priorities, independent action order, shared opportunities and verified recovery.

**Visual review update:** the founder asks to rethink the first pass's architecture and town layout. The views described below remain first-pass studies; their layout is not adopted. The gameplay and persistence constraints continue to guide the revision.

Dhaniverse needs a town whose shape explains its economy and whose everyday routes become familiar. Build the river landing, market, working streets and addressed homes as one connected place. The player should understand where food comes from, where people work, why a shop occupies its corner, and how to get home without repeatedly consulting a map.

The full multiplayer economic life simulation remains the game: **MANAGE → EARN → SPEND → PLAN**, with all 24 systems. [Canonical rules](11_Canonical_Game_Rules.md) retain authority over balances, clocks, eligibility and ownership. This proposal fills spatial and experience gaps; the new activities and placement rules below are candidates for prototyping. Branding remains undecided. The earlier planning note does not determine production scope; this build can later support public demonstrations and investor pitches.

## What makes this place Dhaniverse

The [documented river and rail history](10_City_Art_Direction.md) supplies the town's structure. A landing made trade possible. Workshops and worker housing grew behind it. Rail access later increased activity toward the east. Express those changes through frontage size, structural rhythm, repairs and conversions. Contemporary improvements can sit on an older quay wall; history does not require converting the retained buildings into medieval cottages.

Use a small set of recognizable features repeatedly: the clear river and stone landing; the market's wide sawtooth roof; substantial pearl walls with charcoal frames and warm joinery; deep, full green canopies; and occupied courtyards behind commerce. Later districts inherit these materials and public-space details while changing building form. The station has a long canopy, the school a learning court, and the freight shed large receiving doors.

Each district needs a primary landmark that helps people find a route. At the start, use one spreading green tree beside the quay square. The market roof identifies work and trade, while the low railway canopy signals the eastern connection. Keep pink blossom sparse enough to help orientation. The world should still be recognizable with signs and UI hidden.

Familiarity should accumulate through a stable room address, known neighbors, a preferred food stop, a practiced commute and a business frontage the player recognizes. Their financial history supplies the narrative: moving into a better room, recovering from a bill, qualifying for a job, or hiring someone who once worked beside them. Different players can succeed through stability, enterprise or community, as the progression docs already describe.

## Town structure and the complete service program


Retain the **36-site program** and the manifest's **560 × 480 m review extent** as planning references. Review the town as overlapping streets and neighborhoods. Service reservations identify functions; they need not become 36 isolated monumental buildings. Smaller services can occupy distinct premises in mixed-use blocks, with their own physical doors and access rules.

The existing manifest remains unchanged in this pass. Any later lot or road revision must be versioned explicitly. Retained building masters and released occupied units are protected; concept art never relocates ownership.

|---|---|---|
| Quay square | DV-01 arrival/jobs; 02 bank/ATM; 03 market with eight stalls; 04 Common Table; 05 starter rooms; 36 later café/restaurant | An irregular sheltered square beside the landing, occupied frontages, housing immediately behind. The later dining venue has its own role and entrance. |
| Market to station street | DV-06 grocery; 07 clothing/laundry; 08 electronics/telecom | A continuous commercial approach with varied frontage widths and rear servicing. These shops become useful as maintenance and convenience mechanics arrive. |
| Finance frontages | DV-09 broker; 10 pawn/small loans | Connected to market/bank routes. Smaller secure premises, distinct functions and terms; borrowing is never disguised as free recovery. |
| Care street | DV-11 clinic; 12 pharmacy; 13 hospital/emergency; 14 insurance and claims | Clinic and hospital are visibly different facilities. Plan sales and claims occupy **two independently entered premises**, with clear wayfinding between treatment, medicine and filing. |
| Civic approaches | DV-15 city hall/permits; 16 tax/welfare; 17 court/police; 18 labor office | Public approaches connect to the core and care street. Separate public doors keep registration, benefits, disputes and employment understandable. |
| Learning terrace | DV-19 library; 20 school; 21 college | The free library is visible early. School and college share a learning landscape, with different scale and accessible routes up the rise. |
| Residential streets | DV-22 courtyard apartments; 23 garden houses | Connected courts, local thresholds and varied housing conditions. Every unit is a physical, persistent address; quality tiers can exist in different architectural districts. |
| Western craft quarter | DV-24 homes/workshops; 25 craft employment; 26 timber shops/SHG room | Living over working premises, small courts and useful rear yards. A meeting room supports the documented social economy. |
| Eastern station | DV-27 station/platforms; 28 offices/rental rooms | A legible transit forecourt, shops at ground level and offices/rooms above. Rail boundaries remain safe and accessible crossings connect both sides. |
| Southeastern works | DV-29 factory/pay office; 30 warehouse/freight | Freight spur, loading yards and staff entrances. Pay office and canteen approaches sit outside operating yards. |
| Northern supply landscape | DV-31 farm; 32 orchard/barn; 33 reservoir/valve house | Fields, irrigation, managed orchard rows and water infrastructure explain food and water supply. Routes lead toward the market. |
| Resource and recovery edges | DV-34 quarry/resource yard; 35 community garden/recovery | Quarry terrain belongs to the older extraction landscape. The community garden joins daily and recovery routes; a bench is not automatically a healing interaction. |

Food, goods and labor should have visible origins and destinations. Farm and orchard lanes lead to the market's receiving side; warehouse access serves commerce; workshops show inputs and finished goods; station foot traffic supports nearby shops. These routes explain the simulation. Physical delivery gameplay is optional and needs its own design; this proposal does not replace the economic life sim with courier work.

### Streets and useful open space

Begin with a roughly **150 × 130 m review window around the five retained starter anchors**, not a rectangular ground asset. Compose the broader valley beyond it from the outset. Walkable lane widths, service clearances and court sizes remain blockout hypotheses to test with the actual character and camera.

Street edges come from doors, party walls, garden walls, arcades, planted roots and occupied corners. Put receiving and waste behind public frontages. Use the river bend, terrain grade and older paths to explain changes in street direction; the newer station avenue can be straighter. Every stair needs a joined ramp route where the public network requires one.

Assign every substantial open area a purpose: square, shared court, garden, working yard, field, riverbank, catchment or future authored frontage. Do not fill every gap with props. Quiet garden space is useful when its enclosure, access and maintenance are clear. Keep views outward to the next street, wooded rise or transport connection so the starter neighborhood feels part of a larger place.

## Four routes that organize play

| Route | Destinations | Design purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Daily routine | Room → job board/workplace → food/market → bank → room | The core economic loop on foot, with repeated recognizable encounters. Measure actual counted steps and real elapsed time. |
| Discovery and return | Square → quay/bridge → craft or garden lane → housing court → square | A second way home, longer views and places to meet. It joins the public network instead of ending in leftover lawn. |
| Learning and opportunity | Core → library → shop/workshop employment → school → office/station → college | More of the town becomes meaningful as qualifications and planning improve. Buildings can be visible before their interactions ship. |
| Recovery | Home/core → welfare and stress refuge; emergency approach → hospital → pharmacy → claims → home | Help remains findable under reduced mobility. A navigable path alone does not prove that all eligibility and affordability gates are escapable. |

The map must support cash-versus-energy choices once transport ships. A useful bus stop connects housing, employment and institutions; it is not an ornamental shelter. Phones and telecom later save specific trips under their documented conditions. Keep public information available through appropriate physical services so starter players are not given smartphone benefits for free.

Route acceptance uses the **canonical step counter**, not an assumed conversion from metres. Test full energy, low energy, critical health and illness separately. Count movement, waiting and required interactions. Do not infer a viable routine from a short-looking path on an aerial image.

## First session and the first workplace

Keep the documented onboarding grant: **100 coins, basic clothes and a Tier 1 room free for three days**. Show its physical address and landlord bed before asking the player to plan upgrades. The first rent preview describes the end of the free period; it must not charge rent early.

The first session introduces one coherent decision chain: find work, finish the documented **five-minute tutorial shift**, receive a quoted payment, compare food options, consume a purchase, choose how much cash to keep versus save, and return to the same room. The library is the next visible opportunity. The tutorial price example uses a real supply/demand modifier and the canonical tick cap, rather than writing a fake price to the UI.

Identity registration must precede opening a bank account. **Proposed onboarding location alias:** let the arrival desk perform the existing free municipal registration through the city-hall registry. This preserves the identity gate while avoiding an unexplained extra trip. If that alias is not adopted, include the city-hall visit in the measured tutorial route. Formal job registration and entry-level eligibility retain their separate rules.

### Market shift activity proposal

Prototype the stall helper first because it sits inside the player's daily route and has a clear relationship to food, stock and other people. A **normal shift remains 30 real minutes**, with existing attendance, partial-pay, daily caps and completion energy rules. The five-minute tutorial has explicit quoted terms; it does not redefine normal work.

Inside the workplace boundary, offer a few readable activity episodes:

1. **Prepare the stall.** Match a small order list to produce trays and choose an uncluttered counter arrangement. Show what belongs to the employer rather than the player's inventory.
2. **Handle customers.** Read a short order, select an available item and complete a simple handover. Player customers use their actual purchase transaction; ambient visitors cannot invent spendable player revenue.
3. **Manage the next batch.** Inspect incoming stock, notice a price-board change and choose a display priority. Stock increases, sales and shortages appear only when the server commits their underlying events.

These episodes give players something purposeful to do and observe. Existing stall rules allow income while present, so task completion is initially optional: no unapproved clicking quota, extra wage multiplier or AFK forfeiture. Attendance earns the documented wage. Test whether optional activity, local conversation and economic planning make the normal duration worthwhile; a timer-only workplace is an unresolved product risk.

The prototype must not award a second stream of coins or duplicate goods for moving a visual crate. A work order can rearrange employer presentation without creating new supply. Actual production recipes, output rates and any performance scoring require a separate rule decision. Block incompatible study, treatment, dining and second shifts while work is active. Viewing a dashboard or talking to a nearby person need not mutate the economy.

Use authoritative shift terms, presence and elapsed time, a reconnect-safe shift record, and one settlement receipt. Repeated input or lost acknowledgments must never repeat wages, stock changes or purchases. [Runtime architecture](../specs/systems/Runtime_Architecture.md) owns these transaction boundaries.

## Progression and reasons to return

The existing [progression paths](03_Player_Progression.md) provide three kinds of longer ambition: growing quickly through risk, becoming financially stable, or building trust and community. Use the same town for all three. More expensive housing changes comfort, capacity and obligations; it does not place a cultural district above another.

A good session leaves a self-chosen next action: prepare for literacy, build a rent buffer, maintain clothing, compare a business location, or finish a group commitment. Familiar people and places make those goals personal. Persistent addressed homes and businesses give successes and setbacks somewhere to happen.

Treat these as retention hypotheses to test, not funding claims. Observe whether people can explain their last decision, remember their next goal, find the route home, return voluntarily and recover after missing a session. Track tutorial drop-off, first complete routine, voluntary return, time spent idle at work, reconnect failure and abandonment during recovery. Compare players with similar exposure; a rehearsed demonstration is not evidence of long-term retention.

## Art rules for a cohesive game

The current **Godot trees are the foliage baseline**. Keep their large sculpted canopy volume, packed stylized leaf clusters, substantial branching trunks and lime-to-deep-green shading. Vary silhouette and height within that family. Optimize their delivery only after profiling; preserve the recognizable crowns through LOD changes. Street and landmark trees remain protected decoration rather than harvest nodes.

Use pearl plaster/pale masonry, charcoal frames and chestnut joinery as shared materials. Building character comes from structure, roofline, window rhythm, access and servicing. The modern market retains its sawtooth roof; housing retains galleries and its court; the bank retains its substantial public entrance. Shared material response must not make every building a bank with a different sign.

Ground belongs to the scene: softly irregular paving at the quay, maintained garden soil behind homes, compacted work yards, orchard rows and bank planting shaped by water. Grass thins on trodden routes and collects around roots and walls. Use selective flowers and resident planting rather than covering every edge with the same decorative planter.

Daylight uses warm directional light, readable cool shade and gentle contact shadows. Evening keeps blue sky and green canopy color, with warm light at occupied doors and gathering places. Compare every kit under the same two presets. Use tactile simplified surfaces, modest wear and softened edges. The Dragon Quest reference supplies material/light warmth; Dhaniverse supplies the contemporary architecture and economic setting.

Keep working views readable with depth blur off. The character's scale, doorways, stairs, benches and tree roots must agree. The founder's 13 October direction is a medium, angled third-person camera: show the character and nearby streets without steep overhead framing. Test obstruction by buildings and trees, transitions and browser behavior with that view. The concept images' dense plants, water details and lighting are appearance goals, not an instruction to copy their rendering cost.

### Small models that support daily life

Build a reusable library, then compose purposeful clusters. Hundreds of placements can come from a manageable number of good models and variants.

| Cluster | Useful models | Placement rule |
|---|---|---|
| Public threshold | Door recess, joined ramp/stair, drain, lamp, address/sign, noticeboard | Frame the entrance; preserve clear access and viewing space. |
| Market work | Produce trays, sacks, crate variants, scales, hand trolley, shelf, shutter, price board | Place by a real counter or receiving niche; contents follow authoritative state where interactive. |
| Housing life | Bench, bicycle/stand, laundry rail, planter variants, gate, mailbox, shared noticeboard | Support resident routines and distinguish public, shared and private space. |
| Garden ecology | Root edge, low wall, hedge, fern, grass tuft, flower patch, bed border | Place according to shade, water, maintenance and foot traffic. |
| Care and civic | Clear service signs, accessible seating, medicine counter, sheltered waiting area | Prioritize wayfinding; keep emergency and public entrances visible. |
| Productive edges | Loading door, pallet, workbench, tool rack, utility cabinet, yard fence | Explain receiving, production and safe public boundaries. |

The existing prop study can supply candidates. Review their style and placements as a street section; its object count alone is not a quality gate. Decorative crates, laundry and bicycles do not claim implemented inventory, maintenance or transport mechanics.

## Procedural authoring with persistent identity

The desired feeling is a place that grew through many related decisions. [World persistence rules](11_Canonical_Game_Rules.md#7-world-persistence-and-ownership-invariants) require a finite authored shared town. Use constrained procedural tools to produce and refine **authoring candidates**, then curate and publish a fixed world revision.

1. **Terrain and water:** establish catchment, river slope, buildable terraces, banks and protected vegetation before streets.
2. **Permanent connections:** reserve the two crossings, retained anchors, station corridor, emergency approaches and connected pedestrian routes.
3. **Street history:** grow older quay/craft routes around water and grade; add later station access and rear delivery connections.
4. **Blocks and parcels:** form street edges, courts and servicing space. Reject inaccessible parcels, anonymous leftover voids and decorative dead ends.
5. **Building grammar:** select a construction family and a function-specific footprint, roof and entrance arrangement. Apply transitions gradually across adjoining blocks.
6. **Threshold and ecology grammar:** place props at semantic anchors such as entry, receiving, seating and garden; grow foliage around those spaces without blocking routes or essential views.
7. **Curate and validate:** review walks, silhouettes, land use, recoverability and performance before a candidate becomes published content.

Keep authoritative `site_id`, `unit_id`, service identity, collision and access topology separate from `asset_id`, facade variant and cosmetic seed. Derive cosmetic choices from stable parcel/anchor keys so rebuilding another district does not reroll the player's home. A cosmetic rebuild cannot move doors, change walkability, allocate stock or change a lease. Add capacity through new authored units and IDs; preserve occupied ones.

Rendering chunks partition delivery and visibility. They do not create districts, prices or ownership. Harvestable node state comes from finite server stocks and growth ticks; the client never infers resource availability from how lush a tree looks. Keep future AI dialogue and optional achievements outside the command authority, as the runtime design already requires.

## Rule gaps that block a convincing routine

These require explicit resolution or measurement before production relies on them. They do not authorize a silent balance rewrite.

| Gap | Why it matters | Next decision or check |
|---|---|---|
| Normal shift activity and attendance evidence | A 30-minute presence timer may dominate a session without enough play. | Test the stall proposal at full duration; retain canonical pay until a separate change is adopted. |
| Casual daily cash flow | At an illustrative healthy base wage of 40 coins, one normal stall shift minus 40 rent and an 8-coin wrap is **−8 coins**, before other costs. | Simulate prices, pay modifiers and eligible welfare; decide what routine the game expects after the free days. |
| Rest versus time drain | Tier 1 recovery alone gives 0.125 bars per two minutes: 1 to 5 bars takes **64 minutes**, before competing drain. | Define drain interaction and simultaneous activity rules before displaying a recovery ETA. |
| Complete zero-cash recovery | Emergency-on-account pays for hospital access, but illness cure also needs medicines; Tier 0 disables idle recovery. | Trace mobility, welfare eligibility, medication affordability and return-to-work together. Decide the missing bridge instead of assuming the hospital rule solves everything. |
| Registration before banking | Bank access requires registered identity. | Adopt the explicit arrival registry alias or include the city-hall route. |
| Fractional resources and score cold start | Food, stress and reputation effects are fractional; Financial Health Score normalization and zero-income handling remain incomplete. | Preserve precision; define score windows and empty-history behavior. Bank transfers must not become new income or expenses. |
| Remote shift energy | Transport S13 says 0.5 bars; canonical §3.5 says every completed shift costs 1. | Apply current canonical precedence; record whether a distinct remote exception is intended before remote work ships. |
| Course totals and exceptions | Postgraduate 45 minutes conflicts with five ten-minute sessions; library courses have a specific energy exception. | Reconcile the postgraduate owner row and use course-specific records rather than copying the catalog summary. |
| Event and market examples | The surplus/flood example claims +10%; multiplicative 1.5 × 0.6 is **0.9, or −10%**. Older trade supply wording also conflicts with canonical supply state. | Correct consumer examples through their owners. Never introduce a second price formula. |
| Recovery and transit waiting clocks | Rest, dining, bus waiting and offline timers have different exceptions. | Write an activity compatibility/timer matrix and test it against the canonical clock. |

The map can reduce friction and make recovery understandable. It cannot cure an unaffordable economy or contradictory timer rules by being attractive.

## Production sequence and demonstration

| Step | Deliverable | Evidence needed to continue |
|---|---|---|
| Connected blockout | Retained five anchors, real terrain/bank joins, public/service routes, twelve room addresses, learning and recovery approaches | Walk every connection at actual player scale; record counted steps, time, sightlines and obstructed approaches. |
| One dressed street section | Quay/market edge, housing threshold, shared foliage/materials and daylight/evening | Ground and elevated views read as the same place; service silhouettes and clear paths survive without signage. |
| Connected everyday choices | Server-backed work, food, bank, lease/rest and recovery; routine walkthrough plus alternate permitted orders | Durable receipts, no duplicate payouts/purchases or unit allocation; canonical clocks and quoted terms remain consistent; no tutorial-order gate on otherwise eligible actions. |
| Recovery and longer session | Full 30-minute work, due obligations, treatment/medicine/welfare and return-to-work | Successful recoveries from critical health, illness, eviction and zero cash with their actual costs and eligibility. |
| Different paths in a shared economy | Minimal library qualification with a real additional role; multiple players use common stock, prices, one trade method, homes and later employment | Players can choose different priorities; a committed purchase changes shared state; concurrent attempts cannot sell or allocate the same unit twice; essential independent play remains viable. |
| Wider progression | Library/shop/workshop → school/office; clothing maintenance; devices/transport; business, SHG and ownership | Each added place supplies a tested decision and preserves existing world identities. |

For the next implementation, build the connected quay/market/housing blockout and prototype the market shift in parallel with the transaction store. Final shop meshes follow their neighborhood massing review. The approved art pipeline still governs production integration; concept art does not pass those gates automatically.

A future presentation should show the whole town briefly, then enter one actual routine: work, receive money, make a consequential purchase, save or retain cash, return home, reconnect and show a recoverable setback. Demonstrate another player's effect on shared stock/prices. Use the canonical tutorial exception for a short demonstration; show normal-duration behavior separately. Label unavailable systems and presentation-only scenery clearly.

Measure the browser build against the existing [frontend production contract](../specs/frontend/3D_Frontend_Production.md): 720p/30 fps on the documented target class, cold payload and visible-scene budgets remain provisional until captured. Preserve the full tree silhouette through selective LOD, instancing and background simplification rather than assuming the aerial density fits the browser.

The strongest pitch evidence will be a coherent working game and observed player behavior. The award history establishes a prior result; these images are a design proposal, and future traction must come from the playable build.
